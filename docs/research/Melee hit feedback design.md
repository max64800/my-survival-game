# Readable stagger payoffs make hits land

A hit feels strong when several small signals arrive together and the monster visibly changes state. Short freezes, camera shake and flashes cover the first tenth of a second. The bigger payoff comes from a stagger the player can see, plan around and cash in. Across the sources, **hit-stop runs about 8 to 20 frames** in Super Smash Bros. (**0.13 to 0.33 s at 60 frames per second**), which is close to what we already run (0.06 s light, 0.11 s heavy, about 4 and 7 frames). So the freeze is not our gap. The gap is the stun: the games that feel best make the stagger meter fill fast enough to feel, show it, and reward the player with a big hit while the target is open. Valheim, Elden Ring and Sekiro each do this in a different way. For the owner's "too many hits" problem, the evidence points to faster, weight-based filling, a visible meter and a clear payoff, not longer stuns. Stun-lock is the danger, and Conan Exiles shows it: its developers removed stun breaks in 2023, and by 2026 players said enemies were too easy to stagger. Our game should limit how often one monster can be stunned, not how quickly it can be stunned.

## Freezes and shake cover only the first tenth of a second

Smash Bros. has the most published hit-stop numbers. Its formula scales the freeze with damage: a 15% move gives **8 frames in Melee, 10 in Brawl and Smash 4, and 15 in Ultimate**, and Melee caps the freeze at **20 frames** while Brawl caps it at **30** ([SSBWiki Hitlag](https://ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). The same page says **attacks with no knockback cause no freeze to the target**, and that shielding cuts the freeze to 0.67 of its length. That rule is worth copying: a jab that moves nothing should not freeze anything. Our 0.06 s and 0.11 s values sit at the short end of Smash's range, so the freeze is already doing its job. Valheim's freeze numbers were not found in any source. A mod author says the freeze is already in vanilla, and that heavy attacks can take a longer freeze while light attacks stay quick, because too much freeze makes weapons feel sticky ([Goo's Combat Overhaul](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/gnls/GoosCombatOverhaul/), secondary).

Camera shake is harder to pin to numbers. Vlambeer's Jan Willem Nijman described a shake that pushes the camera **slightly in the opposite direction of the player's gunfire** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). A later recreation of his talk reports a camera kick of **6 px** and **4 added to the shake value**, and says the effect fades quickly when overused ([DK Liao breakdown](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects); secondary). One game-feel primer adds that a freeze should hold both attacker and target, or the enemy looks like it hiccupped, and that a light 2D shake is **1 to 2 px decaying over about 100 ms** ([uhiyama-lab](https://uhiyama-lab.com/en/notes/unity/unity-game-feel-hit-feedback/)). Our rule of shaking only the hitter's camera is the right co-op choice. Shaking every player's screen for every hit is what the "most indie games shake on every hit, which dilutes it" warning is about.

## Visible stagger meters turn several hits into one payoff

Stagger meters differ in three ways: whether the player can see them, what fills them, and how long the target stays open. The table below lists the numbers found; each row's source is linked in the text that follows.

| Game | Meter | What fills it | Payoff while open | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Valheim | Hidden; players at **40% of max health** (community) | Stagger equals damage dealt (one wiki snippet) | Target takes **2x damage** and cannot act | Decay of **20% per second** (unconfirmed) |
| Enshrouded | Visible white bar under health | Parries, blocks and attacks on a blocker | A full bar stuns for **about 5 s** (search snippet) | Not found |
| Elden Ring | Hidden poise: **15 to 65** for normal enemies, **80 to 120** for most bosses | Poise damage per attack | Stagger, and the riposte critical on a weak spot | Enemy regen starts after **base poise / 13 seconds**, then **13 per second**; player poise needs **30 s** with no hits |
| Sekiro | Visible posture bar | Deflects and hits | Guaranteed Deathblow inside a short window; hits do more damage while broken | Recovers after **about 1 s** without posture damage, and not while attacking |
| 7 Days to Die | No meter; a chance per attack | Chance on power attacks (Brawler: **20 to 30%**) | Stun lasting **4 to 8 s**, slowing movement **80%** and attacks **50%**, fading to 20% and 0% | Zombies get up "a few seconds later" |

Valheim's design is the simplest to copy. Because a stagger adds the damage dealt, a big hit and a string of small hits both fill the bar, so the player's effort maps directly to a result ([gamever damage guide](https://gamever.io/knowledge-base/how-valheim-actually-calculates-damage-and-why-your-numbers-swing-so-much), per a wiki snippet). Its weakness showed up in players' own reports. On the hardest setting, one player said "blocking = death," because the block check runs before armour and the stagger check runs after it, so blocks stagger too easily ([Steam thread, Aug 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/0/3827550531058812597), community claim with no developer reply). Hidden meters keep the screen clean, but they make the rules hard to learn. Sekiro's visible posture bar solves that. The player sees the bar climb, plans a Deathblow, and gets a readable reward. Elden Ring's hidden poise sits between the two, and its enemy regeneration rule is a neat tuning lever: a 65-poise enemy waits 5 seconds before its poise starts to refill, and a 120-poise boss waits about 9.2 seconds ([Elden Ring Wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance), fetched). Hidden meters work well for a co-op game where one screen is shared, but the meter needs at least a small visual cue, such as a flash when it is nearly full.

## Knockdowns and stun-lock: the limits that stop chains

Stun-lock is what happens when a target never gets a turn, and the sources show three ways to stop it. The first is to tie armour to the attack, not the monster. Conan Exiles calls this **hyper armor**: "a window of time during an animation in which a character cannot be stunned." Its June 2023 developer letter said heavy weapons with "a lot of moving mass" should keep most of it, and that fast weapons should be able to **interrupt slow ones**, with a missed interrupt leaving the heavy swing unstoppable ([Conan Exiles dev letter](https://www.conanexiles.com/?p=16687)). The same letter removed the old "stun break," which had given roughly **1 second of freedom for every 3 seconds stunned**, and said enemies would now be either stunnable or immune, with only "certain high difficulty enemies" immune. The lesson came in May 2026. A Funcom forum reply admitted that "enemy staggering has at times been too easy to sustain," then said the change had been "over-corrected" and that players wanted "a bit more hyperarmor back" ([Funcom forum, May 2026](https://forums.funcom.com/t/combat-feedback-after-conan-enhanced-update-may-5th-enemy-stagger-hit-detection-combat-flow-issues/297723)). The post is not marked as staff, and no patch number is given. Funcom tunes this by hand, with no fixed number.

The second way is to give a stunned target a slow, readable recovery. 7 Days to Die's stun is a timed debuff, not an animation lock. Legs can stun a zombie "for a couple of seconds," and torso hits can knock it down, after which it "will get up a few seconds later" ([7DTD wiki, Zombie](https://7daystodie.wiki.gg/wiki/Zombie)). The debuff slows movement and attacks by **80% and 50%**, and those slows fade to **20% and 0%** across a 4 to 8 second stun ([7DTD wiki, Enemy Debuffs](https://7daystodie.wiki.gg/wiki/Enemy_Debuffs)). A fall also stuns a zombie and makes it take **triple damage** while stunned ([7DTD wiki, Zombie](https://7daystodie.wiki.gg/wiki/Zombie)). A fade-out, rather than an instant return to full speed, is the detail that keeps a stun from ending with a sudden fight.

The third way is a timed break for the player. Sekiro's player posture only starts to recover after about 1 second without posture damage, and it cannot recover while the player is attacking, sprinting, grappling or staggered ([Fextralife, Sekiro Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture), fetched). Players in several games have asked for the same thing. Warframe and Path of Exile players asked for short stun protection after the first stun ([Warframe forum](https://forums.warframe.com/topic/27121-why-stuns-are-bad) and [PoE forum](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3677859), search snippets). One Conan player put the point bluntly: "Make them better not cheaper with knockdown and stunlock spam" ([Funcom forum](https://forums.funcom.com/t/does-every-npc-need-a-stunlock-knockdown/9918), search snippet). Dying Light shows the input side: its stagger comes from the player's timing (a block window, a dodge or a grapple), so the player can always tell why a stagger happened ([PC Gamer, Dying Light review](https://pcgamer.com/dying-light-review), search snippet).

## Earned criticals beat random ones

The best-known games do not roll for their big hits. They wait for an opening and pay the player for it. Sekiro's Shinobi Deathblow is **guaranteed** once posture is full and the player acts inside the window, and Elden Ring's riposte is a critical hit on most enemies' weak spots that knocks them back and resets poise ([Fextralife, Sekiro Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture) and [Elden Ring Wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance), fetched). Valheim does the same with set multipliers: the third hit of a combo does **double damage**, a stagger adds **2x**, a parry adds **1.5x to 6x** depending on weapon, and a backstab deals **2x to 6x** ([gamever damage guide](https://gamever.io/knowledge-base/how-valheim-actually-calculates-damage-and-why-your-numbers-swing-so-much) and [gamever parry guide](https://gamever.io/knowledge-base/blocking-parrying-in-valheim-shield-types-the-parry-window-and-stagger)). Each bonus comes from something the player did. Our random 4 to 10% crit on every hit gives a x1.75 bonus that the player cannot see coming. The owner's feeling that stuns are slow, and that big hits are rare, fits this pattern: the random crit adds noise, while the stun opening, which is where a crit should live, is too slow to arrive. The notes did not find a source for how crit multipliers are chosen, so 1.5x to 2x values here are general practice, not findings.

## What our co-op game should take

Our current numbers are a hidden per-monster meter that fills on hit, decays at 8 per second after 2.5 seconds idle, stuns for 1.5 seconds, and gives 2 seconds of immunity afterward. The research points to five changes, and each should be checked on the real meter before tuning. The owner's "too many hits" complaint is the first target. The meter should fill by weight, so that a heavy attack fills a much larger share of the bar than a light one, and a monster should stun on about three heavy hits from a fresh bar. Valheim's rule that stagger equals damage is the simplest version. The second change is to show the meter. A thin bar that appears after the first hit, and a short flash when it fills, gives the player the "it worked" signal that Valheim's hidden bar lacks. The third change is a real stun state. Our monsters currently get only one flinch animation, so a stunned monster should hold a clear stagger pose for the stun's length, then recover at reduced speed that fades back to normal, following the 7 Days to Die pattern. A knockdown belongs only to the heavy finisher on a stunned monster, with a getup of 2 to 3 seconds. The fourth change limits chains. The 2 second immunity should stay, and a monster that has been stunned twice in a short window should get a longer immunity. The immunity must count per monster, not per player, so four players hitting one monster cannot chain stuns faster than one. Heavy wind-ups should also carry short hyper armor, so that light hits cannot cancel them, as Conan's 2023 letter describes. The fifth change is about criticals. The stunned-monster Crush (x2 on the first heavy) should stay as the main big number, and the random 4 to 10% crit should shrink or become pure flavour. The owner should decide which.

Two smaller points come from the sources. Hit-stop values stay as they are, but a longer freeze, 0.15 to 0.2 seconds, could be reserved for the stun and knockdown moments, which still sits under Smash's caps. In co-op, the freeze should be a visual effect on each client, while damage is decided on the host. This is an inference from the sources, since no co-op hit-stop source was found, and it should be tested with the owner in a 2 to 4 player session.

## What the sources do not settle

Several numbers the owner might want are missing from the sources. No millisecond value for a white hit flash was found, and no sound layer or pitch range was sourced. Camera shake has no verified size or decay time beyond the 6 px and 100 ms figures above. Valheim's **20% per second decay** and its 40% threshold rest on a wiki snippet and a forum post, not on a verified page. The parry window of 0.25 s comes from one guide. The 7 Days to Die chances come from a wiki page that warns its perk values may be outdated (Version 2.1). The Enshrouded 5-second stun is from a search snippet. Conan's concussive knockout meter, which is a separate bar from health, has only community test numbers ([Funcom forum](https://forums.funcom.com/t/concussive-damage-explanation-in-3-0-or-knocking-out-thralls-umm-scientifically/209427)). These should be read as directions, not as settings to copy.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that the sources do not solve stun-lock with a single number. Each game that handles it well uses two or three limits together: an animation-level armor window, a timed recovery that fades, and a cooldown that the player can see. For our game, the owner's complaint is best answered by making the stun arrive sooner and be readable, not by lengthening it. A stun that arrives after three heavy hits, with a visible bar and a clear payoff, gives the player a reason to plan. The limit on chains is then a per-monster immunity window that four players share, so the group cannot turn a fight into a loop. Testing this with the owner in a live co-op session, counting hits to stun before and after the change, will show quickly whether the numbers are right.
