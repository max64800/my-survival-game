# Players' first hours, and why they quit

Research report, 2026-11-19. Written for the owner (no coding words without an explanation).

**Words used here**
- **Refund:** Steam gives the money back. Anyone can ask within 14 days if they played **less than 2 hours**.
- **Onboarding:** how a game teaches a new player to play.
- **Hook:** the moment a player thinks "oh, this is fun, I want more."
- **Review summary site:** a website that reads thousands of Steam reviews and lists what people like and dislike.
- **Co-op:** playing together with friends.
- **Host:** the player whose PC runs the shared world.

**How sure is this?** Most numbers come from review summary sites (VaporLens, Gamebrain), which use computers to sort reviews. They are a good guide to *what* people complain about, but their exact numbers are rough. The ranking in Part B is my own count of how many of the games had each complaint.

---

## Part A: The first 2 hours

### Why the first 2 hours matter so much
- Steam refunds are automatic under 2 hours of play ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/90993-steam-two-hour-refund-policy-forces-indie-developer.html)).
  One small game with "Very Positive" reviews still had a **21% refund rate** ([FinalBoss](https://finalboss.io/one-indie-dev-lost-55000-sales-to-steams)).
- In game demos, the **typical player quits after about 14 minutes** ([Steam Page Analyzer, using How To Market A Game data](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be)).
- Common advice: the hook must land **within 10 minutes, best within 5**. Players who bounce off a tutorial are the most common loss ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-demo-best-practices), [Wayline](https://wayline.io/blog/crafting-compelling-demo-loops)).
- Microsoft research calls the first hour a **"probation period"**: the player is still deciding if the game is worth it ([CHI Play paper](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/First20Hour20-20CHIPlay20201420-20preprint2.pdf)).
- For free mobile games, over 70% of players never get past the first few minutes ([GDC: The Sticky Science of New User Experiences](https://gdcvault.com/play/1019719/contactUs)). PC players are more patient, but the lesson is the same.

### What the big games do

**Valheim**
- One short, vague goal from a talking raven, then you are free. No long tutorial ([Aywren](https://aywren.com/2021/02/15/first-impressions-of-valheim)).
- Fast early wins: by the end of the first hour, players already have a hut, a cooking fire, weapons and clothes ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/valheim-is-making-me-love-survival-games-again)).
- Food gives *bonuses*, it does not starve you. Critics praised this ([GameSpot: "Valheim succeeds by not starving you"](https://Gamespot.Com/articles/valheim-succeeds-by-not-starving-you/1100-6488320/)).
- Bag weight barely matters in hour one ([CPG guide](https://consolepcgaming.com/valheim-a-true-viking-game-a-guide-on-how-to-survive-your-first-hours-in-the-game)).

**Terraria**
- A helper character (the Guide) is in the world from minute one. Show him an item and he lists what you can make with it. A teacher you ask, not a teacher who lectures ([PC Gamer guide](https://pcgamer.com/terraria-beginners-guide)).
- The first night is the first goal: monsters come out, so you build a shelter. A clear, natural reason to build ([Terraria wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Legacy:Guide:Walkthrough)).

**Core Keeper** (closest to us: underground, digging, co-op)
- Starts in a dark cave next to a big glowing Core. A short intro film, starting items and crafting levels point the way ([PCGamesN, quoted on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Core_Keeper)).
- But guides say the game "never tells you where to go next", and that is why many new players **stall after the first hour** ([XGamingServer guide](https://xgamingserver.com/blog/?p=21852)).
- A review summary site lists **"No tutorial" as the top reason in refund-type reviews** (about 9%) ([VaporLens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1621690/core_keeper/stats/analysis)).
- What players love: the cozy-but-creepy mood and "just one more hour" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/co-op-survival-game-core-keeper-gets-the-mix-of-cozy-and-creepy-just-right), [NoobFeed](https://www.noobfeed.com/articles/core-keeper-early-access-pc-preview)).

**Deep Rock Galactic** (dwarves, caves, co-op)
- Co-op was the *first* design choice, not added later. Every player should feel equally important ([Co-Optimus interview](https://www.co-optimus.com/editorial/1978/page/1/indie-ana-co-op-and-the-deep-rock-galactic-interview.html)).
- Caves mix hand-made pieces with random ones, so they don't feel same-y ([Unreal Engine spotlight](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/how-ghost-ship-games-found-success-with-deep-rock-galactic?lang=en-US)).
- One button makes your dwarf shout "Rock and Stone!" It was made on purpose for players who don't talk on a microphone, and it made the community friendly ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/meet-one-of-the-last-positive-communities-left-in-gaming)).

**Subnautica**
- Almost no tutorial. Some players love the mystery; others say the game is "crying out for a basic tutorial" ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/?p=45789), [Unknown Worlds forum](https://forums.unknownworlds.com/discussion/comment/2317099)).
- The team's own talk is about building curiosity and the feeling of the unknown ([GDC: The Design of Subnautica](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026273/contactUs)).
- In Subnautica 2, players complained the first 10 minutes had too much reading ([Subnautica 2 forum](https://subnautica2.nolt.io/12787)).
- Their designer: a child with *no* tutorial in Astroneer built an amazing base in two hours. Curiosity teaches well if the world is readable ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/survival-crafting/everyones-little-monkey-brains-like-shelter-and-food-subnautica-2-designer-says-primal-instinct-is-the-reason-were-so-drawn-to-smashing-blocks-of-wood-into-weapons)).

**Grounded**
- Opening hours **alone** felt "antagonistic" and like "a chore". With friends, much better ([VGChartz](https://www.vgchartz.com/article/455294/grounded-xs/)).

**Dome Keeper**
- Tiny loop: dig, carry things back home, defend, repeat. The tension of "one more lump of iron before I go back" is the fun ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/dome-keeper-review)).

**GDC talks on teaching players**
- Celia Hodent ("The Gamer's Brain, Part 2", GDC 2016): teach by doing, one thing at a time, at the moment the player needs it ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022951/contactUs), [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-inside-the-ux-of-onboarding-and-player-engagement)).
- Rainbow Six Siege and "Start Right, Start Fun" talks say the same: short, hands-on steps, not text walls ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034684/Start-Right-Start-Fun-Unveiling)).

### The patterns (what the winners share)
1. **Do something fun in the first minute.** Swing, dig, see something glow. No menus or text first.
2. **One small, clear goal, then freedom.** A raven, a guide, a glowing Core. Not a checklist.
3. **Teach when needed, in one line.** Show a hint the first time you pick up a new thing. Never a wall of text.
4. **Quick early wins.** A first tool, a first "home" spot, a first upgrade inside the first 30 to 60 minutes.
5. **Survival is gentle at first.** Hunger and danger grow slowly.
6. **A next goal is always visible.** The #1 early-quit cause is "I don't know what to do now."
7. **Solo must also work.** Many first sessions are alone, even in co-op games.

## Part B: Why players quit or refund

Ranked by how many of the 7 games had the complaint (Valheim, Core Keeper, Deep Rock Galactic, Grounded, Enshrouded, Abiotic Factor, plus general survival-game articles).

| # | Complaint | Seen in | When it hits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grind and repetition** (same tasks over and over) | 6 of 7 | Hours 5+ mostly |
| 2 | **Bad performance** (stutter, low frame rate, crashes) | 3 of 7, but very strong | Minute 1 (refunds!) |
| 3 | **"What do I do now?"** (no direction, no tutorial) | 3 of 7 | First hour (refunds!) |
| 4 | **Co-op problems** (can't join, host leaves, friends lose progress) | 3 of 7 | First session with friends |
| 5 | **Lost saves** | 2 of 7, but makes people rage-quit forever | Any time |
| 6 | **Inventory and hauling pain** (full bag, carrying ore home) | 2 of 7 | Hours 1 to 10 |
| 7 | **Solo too hard** / sudden difficulty jumps | 2 of 7 | First hours |
| 8 | **Hunger/thirst chores** | General complaint across the genre | First hour |
| 9 | **Stamina runs out too fast** | 1 of 7 (Valheim, often) | First hour |
| 10 | **Caves too dark or hard to read** | Other cave games | First hour |
| 11 | **Annoying terrain** (cliffs, slow travel) | 1 of 7 (Valheim Mistlands) | Late |
| 12 | **Weak story or ending** | 1 of 7 (Abiotic Factor) | Very late |

**Details and sources**
1. **Grind.** Valheim's top complaint, getting worse after early areas ([VaporLens Valheim](https://vaporlens.app/app/892970/valheim), [Gamebrain Valheim](https://gamebrain.co/game/valheim): about 10,800 "grinding" mentions). Core Keeper ([Gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/core-keeper)). Deep Rock Galactic: "fun at first but loses appeal once players finish unlocking things" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/548430/discussions/1/3057363849885455041)). Enshrouded: mid- and late-game grind, but early co-op fun praised ([VaporLens Enshrouded](https://vaporlens.app/app/1203620/enshrouded)). Abiotic Factor: too much salvaging ([Gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/abiotic-factor)).
2. **Performance.** Enshrouded went from "Mixed" to "Mostly Positive" reviews only after a fix for crashes and low frame rate ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/steam-reviews-for-new-16-player-survival-rpg-enshrouded-climb-to-mostly-positive-after-an-emergency-server-crash-hotfix-course-corrects-a-rocky-launch)). Core Keeper: stutter where many creatures or lights are ([Gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/core-keeper)). Valheim: stutter and crashes in recent reviews ([VaporLens Valheim](https://vaporlens.app/app/892970/valheim)).
3. **No direction.** Core Keeper (see Part A). Grounded: "doesn't always guide players clearly" ([VaporLens Grounded](https://vaporlens.app/app/962130/grounded)). Subnautica (see Part A).
4. **Co-op problems.** Grounded: host disconnects, friends can't join, non-host players lose progress ([VGChartz](https://www.vgchartz.com/article/455294/grounded-xs/), [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/xbox-one/4-reasons-why-grounded-is-a-win-for-xbox/3/)). Enshrouded: crashes when joining a friend's world ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/steam-reviews-for-new-16-player-survival-rpg-enshrouded-climb-to-mostly-positive-after-an-emergency-server-crash-hotfix-course-corrects-a-rocky-launch)).
5. **Lost saves.** Valheim had a rare "world-destroyer" bug ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/valheim-backup-world-character)); a player lost "20+ hours twice" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/2/3180111158759471338)). Core Keeper: progress vanished when switching PC and Steam Deck ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1621690/discussions/0/599639424473552107)).
6. **Inventory.** Valheim: worn gear takes bag slots, weight limit feels on/off, can't carry ore through portals ([Valheim QoL thread](https://valheim.mmmos.com/view/valheim_general/26SV1), [Games Atlas](https://gamesatlas.com/articles/a-few-small-changes-that-could-improve-valheim)).
7. **Solo too hard.** Grounded is balanced for groups, so solo has "steep difficulty spikes" ([VaporLens Grounded](https://vaporlens.app/app/962130/grounded)).
8. **Hunger chores.** "Hunger meters measure the time until you starve"; many games make them "too demanding right out of the gate" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/how-survival-games-get-hunger-and-thirst-wrong-and-how-to-fix-it), [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/survival-games-are-annoying/)).
9. **Stamina.** Valheim: drains fast, comes back slowly ([VaporLens Valheim](https://vaporlens.app/app/892970/valheim)).
10. **Dark caves.** Sons of the Forest caves called "overly dark"; Valheim nights "too dark" for some ([r/valheim dev post](https://redlib.cypherpunktools.com/r/valheim/comments/lkey2w/common_issues_and_possible_solutions/gookueo)). An indie tip: start caves well lit, get darker later ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/12522658)).
11. **Terrain.** A group of 3 thought about quitting after one day in Valheim's cliffy Mistlands ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/0/3763353492971247638)).
12. **Story.** Abiotic Factor's ending called flat ([VaporLens Abiotic Factor](https://vaporlens.app/app/427410/abiotic_factor)).

**Big lesson:** inside the 2-hour refund window, the killers are #2 (runs badly), #3 (don't know what to do), #4 (co-op breaks), #8 to #10 (chores and darkness). Grind (#1) hurts reviews later, not refunds.

## What this means for our game

Ranked from most to least important. Each one is tied to our dwarf game.

1. **Keep the game smooth from minute one.** Bad performance is the fastest refund. Keep the frame-rate work going (the performance plan in this folder). Test on a medium PC, not only the gaming PC. Never let the first cave stutter.

2. **First minute: swing the pickaxe.** The player should dig rock, see chunks fly into the bag and the lantern glow, all within 60 seconds. No text screens or long menus before this.

3. **Make "lost with a torch" a fun game, not a punishment.** The owner wants players to get lost, and that's a strength. Make it fair:
   - **Torches you place are breadcrumbs.** They stay lit forever, so you can follow your own trail home (like Hansel and Gretel's pebbles).
   - **Torch colours.** Crystal fuel gives different light colours. Let players pick a colour for "the way home" and another for "ore here".
   - **Home glows.** The base fire or bed gives a soft light you can see from far down long tunnels.
   - **Sounds help.** A stream, wind in a shaft, a hum near home. Ears guide you when eyes can't.
   - **Never pitch black.** Keep the dim glow when fuel runs out (already planned). Being lost should feel spooky, never blind.
   - **A "found my way back" moment** feels great. Maybe a little cheer from the dwarf when you reach a torch you placed.

4. **Always a next goal on screen.** One small line, like "Find copper" then "Light the way to the second cavern". The #1 first-hour quit reason in Core Keeper was "no direction". Show one goal at a time. Never a long list.

5. **Teach with one-line hints, only when needed.** First time you pick up a crystal: "Press R to feed your lantern." First time your lantern is low: a small flicker and the hint. No tutorial level.

6. **A first "home" within 30 minutes.** Like Valheim's first hut. A small place to set the first torch, a bed, a chest. Players need an early win they built themselves.

7. **Co-op must "just work".** Joining a friend should take two clicks and never crash. Friends (not only the host) must keep their dwarf's progress. Test joining often. Grounded and Enshrouded were hurt by this.

8. **Make saves impossible to lose.** We already plan autosave every 5 minutes plus backups. Add: never write over the only save (save to a new file, then swap), and keep a few older copies. One lost save can lose a fan forever.

9. **Keep hunger and thirst gentle and rewarding** (already planned: never deadly, food gives boosts). Keep it that way. Don't add hunger in the first 20 minutes at all. Water and mushrooms should be easy to find in Mossy Hollows.

10. **Big pickup radius and a roomy bag** (already planned). Add early: a way to send stuff home or a "deposit everything" button at a chest. Hauling ore is a top Valheim complaint.

11. **Make solo fun too.** The first session is often alone. Monsters in the first area should be easy for one dwarf. Bigger fights can grow with more players.

12. **Stamina: generous early.** Running, digging and jumping in the first hour should rarely run out. Valheim players hate stamina running dry.

13. **Make caves different from each other.** Deep Rock Galactic mixes hand-made pieces with random caves. Hand-made "special spots" (ruins, a crystal grotto, a waterfall) keep exploring fresh and beat grind later.

14. **A "Rock and Stone" style button.** One key for a dwarf cheer or shout. Fun for friends who don't talk on a microphone. Cheap to make, big for co-op mood.

15. **Plan against late grind early.** Each new biome should bring a new trick (new ore tool, new light type, new way to travel), not only "same thing, bigger numbers". Grind is the #1 complaint long-term.

**Not recommended:** a long tutorial, a full map from the start, or removing darkness. They fix "getting lost" by removing the fun part.
