# Shrink the red glow, show hurt elsewhere

Our red edge glow is bigger than it looks. The shader fades the colour in from every edge across about a third of the screen, and it stays at full strength for the whole fade. That is why the full-border ideas and the plain red flash both missed: the first covers too much, and the second is a single loud colour with no meaning. The games that feel good when you get hit mostly use small signals that are not on the picture at all: a health bar with a lagging segment, a quick flash on the character, a small camera nudge, a muffled sound, and a rumble pulse. Accessibility guidance from Microsoft asks for an option to turn off shake and blur, and players who ask for an option to turn down a red overlay usually ask for "more transparent," not removed. We recommend shrinking the red glow to a thin band for big hits only, and moving the main "you got hurt" message to the health bar, the dwarf's body, sound and rumble. Prototype the bar and body first, because they change nothing on the picture. Give every effect its own slider, and never use red as the only signal.

## Our current glow covers far more of the screen than it looks

The red edge glow lives in `src/ui/screen_edge_flash.gd` and `src/ui/screen_edge_flash.gdshader`. The shader measures how far each pixel is from the nearest screen edge, where 0 is the edge and 0.5 is the centre. It then sets the glow to `(1 - distance / 0.35)` squared. That means the tint reaches **35% of the way in from every side**, so only the middle box about **30% wide and 30% tall** stays fully clear. The glow is strongest at the edge and fades with a curve, which makes the outer ring look thinner than it is. A hit starts the glow at **0.35 strength**, rises to **1.0** for bigger hits, and fades out over **1.4 seconds**. The poison, burn and chill glows (green, orange, blue) hold at **0.35 strength for as long as the status lasts**, so a poisoned dwarf wears a steady tint on every edge. We think this steady tint adds to the "too much" feeling, but we did not test it in play. The values above were read from the code, which is **verified**. We did not trace every place that calls the hit flash, so how often it fires in real fights is **not verified**.

The rest of the hit feel is small and already in place. The game freezes both fighters for **0.06 seconds** on a light hit and **0.11 seconds** on a heavy hit (`src/combat/monster_manager.gd`, lines 1443 and 1444), which is in the same range as the **60 to 120 milliseconds** a Godot developer calls "plenty" in [saltmire's Godot 4 hit-stop article](https://dev.to/saltmire/godot-4-screen-shake-and-hit-stop-in-one-script-11eh). The player's camera gets a shake of **0.25 on a light hit and 0.45 on a heavy hit**, and that shake drains at **4 units per second** (`src/player/dwarf.gd`, line 483). These are the strongest existing hurt signals, and they already work without the screen glow. Only the glow is the problem.

## Other games signal hurt with small, separate cues

The cleanest verified example is Minecraft. Its wiki says a hurt mob or player turns **red for 10 game ticks, which is 0.5 seconds**, and the health bar gets a white outline that flashes for **1 second** on Java ([Minecraft Wiki, Damage](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Damage)). The same page says the screen **tilts toward the attacker**, and that players can turn the tilt off in the accessibility options. Halo Infinite shows hurt on the shield bar at the top of the screen, which flashes red when the shield runs out, and the shield comes back after about **5 seconds** without damage ([Halo Waypoint support](https://support.halowaypoint.com/hc/en-us/articles/24284987877524-Shields-in-Halo-Infinite)). Naughty Dog's The Last of Us Part II gives separate controls for "Full Screen Effects," "Damage Indicators" (which put a spike on the HUD in the direction of the hit), "HUD Flashing," a **1 to 10 camera shake slider**, and combat vibration ([Naughty Dog, accessibility features](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/THE_LAST_OF_US_PART_II_ACCESSIBILITY_FEATURES_DETAILED)). That is the split we want: the screen effect is one option among several. Halo 3 went further and replaced Halo 2's full-screen flash with a directional gradient plus small red arrows, according to a summary of its design notes ([Halopedia HUD](https://www.halopedia.org/HUD), secondary source).

The **ghost bar** (a pale second segment that lags behind the health bar and drains after a hit) is the most promising HUD idea. Street Fighter Alpha 3 uses one, Tekken 8 has a grey "recoverable health" bar, and Windrose uses a "temporal health" segment that disappears if you do not win it back in time ([Esports.gg, Tekken 8](https://esports.gg/guides/tekken-8/health-in-tekken-8-explained); [Allthings.how, Windrose](https://allthings.how/temporal-health-in-windrose-explained/)). We only saw these in search summaries, so the exact timings are **not verified**. Minecraft and Valheim mods also show how little it takes. Wire's Damage Vignette, a Valheim mod, uses a red edge flash with an opacity of **0.20** and a duration of **0.60 seconds**, and starts a low-health vignette below **65%** health. Its opacity and duration are adjustable ([Thunderstore, Wire's Damage Vignette](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Wire/WiresDamageVignette/)). The **0.20 and 0.60 seconds** are the mod's defaults, read from the mod page.

## Players reject lingering red, shake and blur, not short cues

The complaints are consistent. A Kotaku article called the red low-health screen "de rigueur game design for ages," and argued that the effect adds no information when a health bar is already on screen, and that low health makes enemies hard to see, which the writer called "an ouroboros of failure" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/enough-with-the-red-screen-of-almost-death-1846752628)). One other source conflicts: the team's opinion note says that article could not be opened, so treat the Kotaku quotes as reported, not checked. Forum users on Tildes described the same frustration, and one defended a red screen as "one of the best solutions" for danger anywhere on screen, because audio and health bars get missed ([Tildes thread](https://tildes.net/~games/wee/enough_with_the_red_screen_of_almost_death)). Vintage Story players asked to turn off a damage overlay because "the sudden red flash is very harsh on sensitive eyes," and asked for it to be "more transparent instead of removed entirely" ([Vintage Story forum, overlay](https://www.vintagestory.at/forums/topic/1471-option-to-disable-damage-overlay-1122/)). The developer's only answer was a file edit that sets the overlay to 0.9 or 2, not a setting. The nausea complaints are sharper. A Vintage Story player said it "can be very nauseating to watch your view pan by itself when on low health" ([Vintage Story forum, camera drift](https://www.vintagestory.at/forums/topic/7302-ability-to-turn-off-camera-drift-when-low-health-for-people-who-get-motion-sick)). Overwatch 2 players said beta shake was "way overkill" and made them sick, and one found that a "reduced" setting only cut the shake from damage, not all shake ([Blizzard forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/overwatch/t/overwatch-2-accessibility-and-screen-shake/671231)).

The praise is for short, directional cues. A Giant Bomb entry says a damage flash "helps inform the player that they are receiving damage as well as the direction it is coming from" ([Giant Bomb, Damage Flash](https://giantbomb.com/damage-flash/3015-3282/)). The Medium UX analysis says a plain flash gives only vague direction, so it is "usually paired with a directional element" (search summary only, [Medium](https://medium.com/@jasper.stephenson/a-ux-analysis-of-first-person-shooter-damage-indicators-59ac9d41caf8)). For our melee game, direction matters less. Signals and Light notes that off-screen attacks are the hard case, and that an arrow "doesn't tell the player what kind of attack it is," so we would show an arrow only for attackers off-screen ([Signals and Light](https://signalsandlight.substack.com/p/how-do-enemy-attacks-work-with-the)). Dead Space shows health on Isaac's spine, which keeps the eye on the centre of the screen. This approach is documented in a [Dead Space 2 interface analysis](https://www.hudsandguis.com/home/2012/08/22/dead-space-2-diegetic-interface-design), but we did not find numbers.

## Safety limits cap any flash, and the sources disagree slightly

Any full-screen flash must pass photosensitivity rules, and our red glow is already close to the limit. Xbox's guideline 118 says a flash is a **10% change in brightness**, fails if it happens **more than about three times per second**, and fails if it covers **about 20% or more of the screen**. A red flash has its own, lower bar, and the guideline's fix is to "desaturate the red coloring" ([Xbox XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)). The Game Accessibility Guidelines add that a flash lasting **more than 5 seconds** fails, and give **25%** for moving patterns and **40%** for static ones ([Game Accessibility Guidelines, flicker](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-flickering-images-and-repetitive-patterns/)). WCAG 2.3.1, the web standard, allows no more than three flashes in any one-second window, and it uses a different area rule ([W3C, WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). These numbers disagree slightly on area, so we use the strictest one, **20%**, as our team limit. A single hit fade that is slow and not red is very unlikely to fail, but we should test it with a flash-testing tool anyway. The Xbox guideline lists the Harding FPA tool for this ([hardingfpa.com](https://www.hardingfpa.com/)).

Two more facts shape the design. Xbox guideline 117 says games should "avoid the use of camera shake, camera bobbing effects, motion blur, mouse blur, and more or provide an option to turn off these behaviors" ([Xbox XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)), so every shake and nudge needs a control. Halo Infinite is the example it gives, with a **0 to 100% slider** each for radial blur, screen shake and full-screen effects. Low-health thresholds are less settled. Mod authors cluster around **25%** for a heartbeat or pulse, and Valheim's Low Health Indicator mod makes the threshold configurable ([Thunderstore, Low Health Indicator](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/YouDied/Low_Health_Indicator/)). The 25% figure is a mod default, not a studio number.

## Five small options to prototype, in this order

The first two options change nothing on the picture, so they are the safest place to start. Option 1 is the only change to the current red glow, and it should be tested after the others, so we can compare it with the owner's reaction.

| Option | Starting values | Why it is subtle | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. HUD ghost bar | A pale segment (white, about 60% alpha) after the health bar. Holds **0.6 s**, then drains over **0.4 s** (ease-out). Use real time, so hit-stop does not freeze it. | Changes only a corner of the HUD. Shows how much was lost. | Ghost-bar idea: search summaries only. Numbers: suggested. |
| 2. Dwarf body flash and lean | Pale tint on the dwarf model only, about **0.05 s** (2 to 3 frames at 60 fps), fades back over **0.15 s**. Optional lean of **0.2 s**, heavy hits only. Reuse the monster hit-flash pattern already in `monster.gd`. | The signal is on the character, not the picture. | Suggested. A "2-frame white flash" claim is one secondary article ([freegamesprites](https://freegamesprites.com/es/news/game-feel-2d-combat-juice-tricks)), not verified. |
| 3. Small red edge glow, big hits only | Change `0.35` to **0.10** in the shader (band about 10% from each edge). Cap hit strength at **0.35** (not 1.0). Fade **0.4 s** instead of 1.4 s. Only for hits over **10% of max health**. Lower the poison/burn/chill glow from **0.35 to 0.15**. | Thin band, short, and rare. Keeps the red cue the owner already saw. | Current 0.35 and 1.4 s values verified in code. New numbers: ours, to test. |
| 4. Camera nudge with a slider | Keep the existing shake (**0.25** light, **0.45** heavy, which is already in `monster_manager.gd`). Add a slider, default **50%**. Reduce-motion turns it off. | Small and under the player's control. | Current values verified. Slider follows the Xbox guideline. |
| 5. Sound muffle and rumble | Heavy hits only: world sounds through a low-pass filter (a filter that lets only low sounds through) at **1000 Hz**, back to **2000 Hz** over **0.3 s**. Rumble: light hit **0.15** weak for **0.08 s**. Heavy hit **0.3** weak and **0.6** strong for **0.18 s**. Only the player who was hit rumbles. | Sound and touch carry the message, not the picture. | Godot's 2000 Hz default is from the [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_audioeffectlowpassfilter.html). Rumble call from the [Godot Input docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_input.html). Other numbers: suggested. Sounds must be real CC0 recordings, filtered. |

For low health, we do not recommend a screen effect at all. Use a HUD-only pulse on the health bar at **25% of max health**, switch it off above **30%** so it does not flicker, and add an optional heartbeat sound at the same point. The pulse must not change the colour of the picture.

The settings we should offer follow the Xbox and Halo patterns. The table lists our starting defaults.

| Setting | Default | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hurt glow on screen | Small | Off, Small, Normal | Off removes the red edge glow. |
| Camera nudge | 50% | 0 to 100% | Matches the Halo Infinite slider pattern. |
| Reduce motion | Off | On or off | Turns off nudge, lean and any tilt. |
| Hit rumble | On, 50% | On or off, plus strength | Only for the player who was hit. |
| Hit sound muffle | On | On or off | |
| Low-health pulse | On | On or off | HUD bar and heartbeat only. |
| Teammate hit display | Nameplate pulse | Off or nameplate | No screen effect for a teammate's hit. |

All flashes must stay under **3 per second**, must never be full-screen red, and each must pass a flash test before release.

## How sure we are, and what is still open

Most of the numbers that describe our game came from reading our own code: the glow shader, the glow and fade timings, the hit-stop and shake values. Those are **verified**. The Minecraft wiki values, the Xbox guidelines, the Game Accessibility Guidelines flicker page, the W3C page, the Naughty Dog settings list and the Halo support page were read on the page itself. The ghost-bar examples, the Halo 3 directional gradient, the Overwatch and Diablo complaints, the Medium analysis and the Crimson Omen cog (Gears of War) came from search summaries or wiki snippets, so treat them as **leads, not facts**. Everything in the prototype table marked "suggested" is a starting guess from general game practice, and we have not tested it. The Kotaku article is a conflict between our own notes: two notes say it was read, and one says it could not be opened. We also did not find any study of how much screen coverage or how long a hurt effect should last, and we did not find how the owner's own players reacted to the red glow. The notes list several games we could not check at all, including Hades, Elden Ring, Dark Souls, Monster Hunter and Terraria.

## Conclusion: prototype the bar and body before touching the glow

The clearest finding is that our hurt feedback is already strong in the places that do not cover the picture. The hit-stop, the camera shake and the red glow each have a job, but the glow is doing the job of three signals at once, and it is doing it across most of the screen. The safest path is to add the ghost bar and the dwarf body flash first, then add the camera slider and the sound and rumble pulse, and only then decide whether the thin red glow still feels necessary. If the owner still wants a red edge flash after the others are in, the thin version in option 3 is a fair middle ground between the rejected full-border options and the plain flash.

For the next step, build options 1 and 2 as a prototype and play-test them against the same three hits: a light hit, a heavy hit, and a hit while poisoned. Ask whether the player noticed the hit without looking away from the fight. If the answer is no, bring back the thin glow from option 3 and test again.
