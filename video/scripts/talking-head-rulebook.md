# Talking Head Communication Rulebook
Oct 14, 2026 · Max Karnaukh (research by Claude)

## How to use this rulebook
**The master rule: every second of a short video must either deliver value or make the viewer need the next second.** The king mattress finding is the clearest example, and almost every rule below is a variation of it.

**Why backstory killed the mattress video.** The hook opened a question ("why is it not a king?"). The backstory neither answered it nor made it more urgent, so the viewer paid the cost of waiting and got nothing back. Viewers don't leave because a video is long. They leave when the next sentence is worth less than the next video in the feed.

There are 55 rules in 7 sections, then platform notes and a worked rewrite. Don't try to apply all of them at once. Pick the 3 that would have fixed your weakest recent video and film with those in mind for a week.

## Pre-record checklist (60 seconds, every video)
- [ ] Can I say the whole point in one sentence of under 15 words?
- [ ] Does the payoff (fact, number, answer) land by sentence 2 or 3?
- [ ] Is there a reason to stay after the payoff: a bigger second point, a twist, or "what to do about it"?
- [ ] Is every number concrete and in units the viewer can feel (cm, AED, minutes, dollars)?
- [ ] Does every "up X%" or "same plot now sells for" compare the same thing at both ends (same release, size, developer vs resale, DLD period)? If anything else changed besides time, say it or drop the %.
- [ ] Did I cut every sentence that's about me rather than about what the viewer gets?
- [ ] Is there one clear thing I want them to do or feel at the end?

## Hooks and the first 3 seconds
Most viewers who leave a Short leave in the first 3 seconds; one analysis puts it at 50-60% of all drop-offs (Opus). Platforms read that early hold as a main signal for pushing a video (PostEverywhere).
1. **Hook with a surprising claim, not the topic.** "Let's talk about mattresses in Dubai" is a topic. "Your king mattress in Dubai is not a king" contradicts a belief, and that's the strongest opener you have.
2. **Open a gap the viewer already cares about.** Curiosity needs some prior knowledge (Loewenstein, information gap). Hook on what they half-know already: rent, visas, mattress sizes, ChatGPT.
3. **Make the gap feel closable, then close it fast.** Curiosity spikes when the answer feels near.
4. **Keep the spoken hook under 10 words,** and repeat it as on-screen text for silent scrollers.
5. **Name the viewer's situation in the hook.** "If you just moved to Dubai..." picks out the right people.
6. **Start mid-sentence, mid-motion.** No "hey guys", no breath, no smile before speaking. Cut a leading "so".
7. **Show a real person or the real object in frame 1.**
8. **Test hooks, not topics.** Record 2-3 hook lines for the same body and compare the 3-second hold.

## Structure and payoff
Use the inverted pyramid, also called BLUF (bottom line up front): answer first, then the most useful detail, then context if there's time.
9. **Payoff by sentence 2 or 3.** Backstory comes after, and only if it adds a second payoff.
10. **Close one loop, open the next.** Once the hook is paid off, open a bigger question: "...and that's not even the worst part." Chain 2-3 small loops.
11. **Never fake a loop.** Pay every promise you make.
12. **Use the ladder: fact, so what, now what.** Most explainers stop at the fact.
13. **One video, one idea.** Three points means three videos.
14. **Find the core first** (Made to Stick). Finish this sentence: "The one thing they should remember is ___."
15. **Plan the last 3 seconds as carefully as the first 3.** End on the strongest line; looping back to the opening line invites a rewatch.
16. **Cut until it hurts, then cut once more.** Tip Shorts hold best at about 25 s or less.

## Storytelling
A story has to come after the payoff, or be the payoff. For every sentence, ask whether it raises tension or releases it.
17. **And, But, Therefore (ABT).** Agreement, contradiction, consequence (Randy Olson, from the South Park writers' rule).
18. **Kill "and then" scripts.** Read the script out loud and count the "buts".
19. **Backstory only as a "but".** "We moved here 12 months ago" is an "and". "We spent 4,000 AED on the wrong mattress" is a "but".
20. **Tension must rise, not stay flat** (Zak).
21. **Put a person with stakes in it.**
22. **Be the guide, not the hero.** Your story proves you've been there; then turn back to the viewer.
23. **Use your scars:** 40,000 cold calls, Vancouver to Dubai, vibe coding with no coding background.
24. **Show the before and after.**

## Language and word choice
Simple words make you look smarter (Oppenheimer).
25. **Use the viewer's word.** Swap jargon for what a smart 14-year-old would say.
26. **Beat the curse of knowledge.** Explain the script to Snow and note every place she frowns.
27. **Concrete beats abstract, always.**
28. **Translate numbers into human scale.**
29. **Round for the ear.** Keep the exact figure on screen.
30. **Talk to one person, as "you".**
31. **Short sentences, active verbs.**
32. **Use "but" and "because".**
33. **NLP: keep the useful parts, drop the claims.** Match the viewer's words, paint pictures, frame things as gains. Skip "embedded commands".

## Delivery: voice, pace, eyes, energy
34. **Brisk, conversational pace:** about 3.5 words per second. Jump cuts remove dead air but shouldn't make you sound rushed.
35. **Keep some pauses.** A short pause right before the payoff number is the most powerful moment in a clip.
36. **Look into the lens.** Put a dot or sticker next to it.
37. **Don't overdo the pitch swings.** Aim for a friend at dinner, not a radio ad.
38. **Land sentences down.** End the payoff line on a falling tone.
39. **Raise energy about 20% above normal, not 100%.**
40. **Let your hands work:** size, sequence, contrast.
41. **Frame for the face.** Eyes in the top third, camera at or slightly above eye level, bottom third clear for captions.

## Persuasion and selling
On video you're selling the next 5 seconds, then the follow, then the DM. Cialdini's seven principles are the map.
42. **Give first.** Every video hands over one thing the viewer can use today.
43. **Show proof, don't claim it.** Put the credential in one clause, where it supports a point.
44. **Build "we".** Name your tribes: expats in Dubai, solo salespeople, non-coders building with AI.
45. **Raise the objection yourself, then answer it.**
46. **Never raise a doubt and leave it hanging.**
47. **Use social proof carefully,** and only if it's true. Accuracy is your brand.
48. **One CTA, matched to the video.**
49. **Make the CTA a payoff, not a toll.**

## Visuals and editing for retention
70-85% of short-form is watched with the sound off or low.
50. **Burn in captions, always.** Make them large and high-contrast, clear of the buttons.
51. **Change something every 2-4 seconds.**
52. **Animate the number, not the decoration.**
53. **Show the real thing.**
54. **Use the first frame as a thumbnail:** face mid-expression plus 3-6 words of text.
55. **Design for the DM share.** Ask: who would someone send this to?

## Platform notes and metrics to watch
| Platform | Diagnoses the hook | Diagnoses the body | Extra signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Viewed vs. swiped away (aim for 70-80% viewed) | Average % viewed, retention curve | Subscribers gained per Short |
| Instagram Reels | 3-second hold | Watch time | Sends per reach (DM shares) |
| TikTok | Retention in the first 3 s | Average watch time, % watched in full | Shares and saves |

**How to read a bad video:**
- A cliff in the first 3 s means the hook failed (rules 1-8).
- A steady slide in the middle means the payoff came too late or the loops stopped (rules 9-16).
- A drop right before the end means a weak or predictable close (rule 15).

**Weekly habit:** pull the best and worst video of the week, play both with the retention graph open, and write one sentence on the exact second people left and why.

## Worked rewrite: the king mattress video
1. **Hook (0-3 s):** "Your king mattress in Dubai is not a king." (on screen: KING? with a tape measure)
2. **Payoff (3-7 s):** "It's about 20 centimetres smaller than a North American king." (an animated bar shrinks)
3. **New loop (7-10 s):** "And that's not even the expensive part."
4. **Stakes as a "but" (10-20 s):** "We brought our king sheets from Canada, BUT they hang off the sides like a tent..."
5. **Objection and answer (20-25 s):** "You're thinking: just ask for a US king. Most stores here don't stock it."
6. **Action and close (25-30 s):** "Measure in centimetres, not names. Send this to anyone moving to Dubai."

## Fill-in template for any topic
- **HOOK** (under 10 words): a claim that contradicts what they believe
- **PAYOFF** (sentence 2): the answer + one concrete number
- **LOOP:** "And that's not the worst part" / "But here's what nobody mentions"
- **BUT:** a cost, mistake or story beat that raises the stakes
- **OBJECTION:** "You're thinking [X]. Here's why not."
- **ACTION:** one thing to do today
- **CLOSE:** the strongest line, or one that loops back to the hook
- **CTA:** one ask (send, comment keyword, or follow for part 2)
