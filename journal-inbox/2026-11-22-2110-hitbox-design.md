## Nov 22 (PC, evening): designing real hitboxes (no code yet)

**What we did**
- Read the two places that decide "did that hit?": `dwarf_fight.gd:_impact` (distance + a slice of a circle in front
  of the dwarf) and `monster_manager.gd:_in_reach` (distance only, +0.8 m). Neither looks at where the weapon really is,
  which is why the boss's spinning pick hit the owner even when it pointed away.
- Wrote a design for shape hitboxes (a box riding on each weapon, a capsule "hurtbox" on each body) and showed it to
  the owner before writing any code.

**Hurdles and mistakes**
- The task said "physics only runs on the host in Godot". That isn't true: every computer runs its own physics.
  The game already splits the work: your own swing is checked on your computer and the host approves it; a monster's
  swing at you is checked on *your* computer (so parry timing is fair). The design keeps that split.
- The task said only the player gets a hurtbox. The dwarf's weapons need something to hit, so monsters need hurtboxes too.
- Many monsters (snails, beetles, bats, spiders) have no skeleton, so "a box on the weapon bone" can't work for them.
  Their box goes on the front of their body instead; spit stays a flying blob; ground slams get a flat round zone.
- A box checked at one single moment would miss a fast spin. The design keeps the box "on" for a short window and
  checks it every physics step, with extra in-between checks for spins.
